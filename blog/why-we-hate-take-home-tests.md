## The take-home test trap

Every developer knows the drill: you apply for a job, pass the initial screen, and then — surprise — you're asked to spend 8 to 20 hours building a toy application that will never see production. The instructions are vague on purpose. The grading criteria are opaque. And if you're rejected, you rarely get meaningful feedback.

## What's wrong with this picture

- **Unpaid labor** — candidates invest significant time with no guarantee of a fair evaluation
- **Bias toward the unemployed** — people with full-time jobs and families can't carve out an entire weekend
- **Artificial constraints** — a take-home test measures how well you build a todo app in isolation, not how you collaborate on real systems
- **No signal about real work** — the best predictor of future performance is past performance, not a contrived exercise

## The 16 Signals approach

Instead of asking candidates to prove themselves with a synthetic test, we look at what they've already built. Real commits. Real pull requests. Real architectural decisions made in production codebases.

Our analysis engine reads the evidence that already exists in their GitHub history and produces a structured, objective brief for the employer. No weekend lost. No guessing. Just real work, fairly assessed.
