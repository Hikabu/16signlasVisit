 Global rhythm: still → still → slow reveal → interactive proof → frozen → dramatic → warm → silent close. Nothing on this page moves twice. Every section gets exactly one motion event; the scarcity is the choreography.

Scene 1 — Hero: The Report, and One Sentence
First noticed: stillness. On a page in this category, the absence of glow is the loudest thing on screen. Then the sentence. Then, half a beat later, the realization that the object beneath it is real.
Focal point: the report artifact — cropped, slightly oversized, entering from the lower third like a document laid on a desk in front of the visitor. The headline sits above it, ink on dark, unaccompanied.
Emotional job: establish the room. The visitor must feel they've walked into a serious practice, not a launch event. Calm authority; the feeling of an expert who doesn't need to raise their voice.
Register: quiet, documentary.
Typography: the largest sans on the entire page — one line, tight, no gradient, no accent color. Below the fold-line of the artifact, the first monospace appears: a timestamp, a citation marker. Sans speaks; mono is already quietly proving.
Product: the report is the hero image. Real content, real density, deliberately cropped so it can't be fully read — legible enough to be real, incomplete enough to demand the scroll.
Evidence: one visible citation marker inside the artifact, already rendered in the evidence accent. The visitor's eye learns the accent's meaning here, before any explanation.
What moves: one thing, once. Two seconds after settle, a single claim line inside the report underlines itself and a hairline thread draws — unhurried, ~350ms — to a small artifact reference. The receipt-pull, performed once, unannounced, like a tell. Then stillness returns.
What never moves: the background. The headline. Nothing ambient, nothing looping, no parallax drift. The dark field is a still surface for the entire scene.
Aha interaction: hovering the report tilts nothing, glows nothing — instead the cursor becomes a subtle examination affordance over citation markers, hinting they're real links. The "aha" is deferred by design; the hero only promises it.
Scroll reveal vs. immediate: headline, artifact, and both CTAs visible immediately — a scanner must leave knowing what this is. The full anatomy of the report is withheld.
Whitespace: enormous above and beside the headline; the artifact is the only dense object. The contrast between empty field and dense document is the brand thesis in one frame.
Pacing: the slowest moment of the page. Let it hold.
Transition: scrolling doesn't slide to a new panel; the report recedes downward slightly, as if set aside on the desk, and the field opens for the argument. The visitor should feel: the artifact will return.

Scene 2 — The Shift
First noticed: the page has become almost pure typography. After the artifact-forward hero, this scene is deliberately spare — a held breath.
Focal point: one short statement in large sans, alone in the frame at first.
Emotional job: quiet recognition. Not outrage at a broken world — the small "huh" of a reframe landing. The visitor should feel a mild temporal vertigo: engineering moved; the room where hiring happens didn't.
Register: editorial. The most essay-like scene on the page.
Typography: carries the entire scene. One large declarative line; a short mono sub-line beneath it that grounds the claim in the texture of the record (the kinds of traces work leaves — rendered as evidence-voice, not marketing-voice).
Product: none. Deliberately. The product's absence here makes its return in Scene 3 feel earned.
Evidence: appears only at the scene's end — see reveal below.
What moves: one reveal on scroll: beneath the statement, a thin horizontal band fades in low-contrast across the width of the frame — years of fine marks, an engineer's work-history strata, barely lit, like a seam of ore visible in rock. It does not animate internally. It simply becomes present.
What never moves: the statement. No triptychs of collapsing resumes, no crossed-out old-world icons — we cut that world from the narrative; the design must not smuggle it back.
Aha interaction: none. This scene is intentionally interaction-free; withholding interactivity here makes Scene 4's interactivity feel like a privilege granted.
Scroll reveal vs. immediate: statement immediate; the evidence band reveals only after the visitor commits to a further scroll — the record literally appears because they looked.
Whitespace: maximal. This is the emptiest scene on the page — a full viewport where typography and one faint band carry everything.
Pacing: identical tempo to the hero — two still scenes in a row is a deliberate risk that establishes the page's metabolism. Anyone bored by stillness was never the buyer.
Transition: the faint band does not disappear on scroll — it brightens and expands into the next scene, becoming its subject. Scene 2's last frame is Scene 3's first.

Scene 3 — What Work Reveals
First noticed: the band, now awake — the same strata from Scene 2, larger, closer, resolved into distinguishable marks: commits, review threads, decisions across years.
Focal point: the timeline itself, and then — sequentially — individual marks being found: highlighted one at a time in the evidence accent, each with a small mono annotation surfacing beside it.
Emotional job: wonder, of the forensic kind. Not "magic AI" wonder — the archivist's wonder of realizing how much a record contains. The visitor should feel they're looking over the shoulder of a careful reader.
Register: documentary shifting into analytical. The scene where the brand's "examination" metaphor is performed in full.
Typography: annotations in mono (the record's voice), section framing in sans kept small and recessive — the evidence outranks our voice here, by design.
Product: implicit only. This is the raw material, not the report — the reading, not the read-out.
Evidence: everything on screen. Three to four found-marks, each representing a different revelation class (a review-response thread; the same system revisited two years apart; an incident; an AI-collaboration trace). Each is specific, timestamped, plausible. Nothing generic survives review here — a fake-feeling mark kills the scene.
What moves: the finding, driven by scroll position: as the visitor scrolls, the examination proceeds — mark found, highlighted, annotated; next mark. The visitor's own scroll is the act of reading the record. This is the section's central design idea: scrubbing through years of a career with your thumb.
What never moves: the timeline's base layer. Marks don't drift, pulse, or float. Found-marks, once lit, stay lit — evidence, once established, does not flicker.
Aha interaction: the scroll-as-examination mechanic itself. Optional hover on any found-mark deepens its annotation — a small reward for the curious, invisible to the scanner.
Scroll reveal vs. immediate: the band and first found-mark immediate; subsequent finds are scroll-earned, one per beat.
Whitespace: above and below the band, generous; the band itself is the page's first honest density.
Pacing: the page's first movement after two still scenes — but adagio. Finds are spaced; nothing overlaps.
Transition: the final found-mark's annotation thread doesn't terminate — it extends downward off-frame, and the scroll follows it into Scene 4, where the other end of the thread is revealed to be attached to a claim in the report. The thread is the literal bridge: record → receipt.

Scene 4 — Every Claim Has a Receipt
First noticed: the report is back — the hero's promise kept — now full-width, sharp, and evidently alive.
Focal point: one claim inside the report, pre-underlined, thread already attached from the scene above. The eye lands exactly where the interaction begins.
Emotional job: the earned "oh." Everything before this was posture; this is proof. The visitor's skepticism about AI-generated confidence is scheduled to die in this scene, by their own hand.
Register: technical, precise — the Linear-est moment of the page.
Typography: the full two-voice system at work: claims in sans, receipts in mono, the accent exclusively on citation elements. This scene is the typography philosophy demonstrated.
Product: the real report at full fidelity — honest density, real metadata, the evidence manifest visible at its head (what was analyzed; what wasn't). The manifest must be visibly present even if the visitor never reads it; its existence is the message.
Evidence: the receipts themselves — actual artifact panels (a PR, a review thread) that surface when claims are pulled.
What moves: only what the visitor moves. This is the page's one truly interactive scene: click or tap any of three to four enabled claims → underline → thread draws → artifact panel surfaces beside the report, ~300ms, precise easing, no bounce. One claim open at a time; opening another closes the last — examination is sequential, not a mess of tabs.
What never moves: the report's frame. Claims never animate unprompted here — after the hero's single demonstration, the receipt-pull now belongs to the visitor. The system never performs it uninvited again.
Aha interaction: the pull itself — "I clicked the claim and landed on the actual pull request." This is the conversion moment for skeptics and the single interaction the entire page exists to deliver. It must work flawlessly on touch.
Scroll reveal vs. immediate: the report immediate and whole; artifact panels exist only behind interaction — proof is available, never pushed.
Whitespace: tighter than anywhere else on the page. Density is honest here; a spacious "airy" report would read as fake UI.
Pacing: the tempo peak of the first half. Where Scenes 1–2 held the visitor still, Scene 4 hands them the controls.
Transition: a hard cut. No thread, no morph — the next scene arrives as a full stop: field empties, type returns. The abruptness is intentional; it frames what follows as a different kind of statement.

Scene 5 — What We Refuse to Do
First noticed: everything stopped. After the interactive scene, this frame is inert — and the visitor feels the temperature drop before reading a word.
Focal point: two (at most three) refusal statements, set large, flat, unadorned. Plain declarations on a still field.
Emotional job: respect. The scene should feel like the moment in an interview when the candidate says "I don't know" and everyone in the room silently upgrades their opinion. Integrity as a physical sensation.
Register: austere. The most severe frame of the page — closer to a legal exhibit than a website section.
Typography: sans, ink, no accent anywhere. The evidence color is deliberately withheld in this scene — there is nothing to cite; that's the point. Its absence, after four scenes of learned association, is itself legible.
Product: one still image only, if any: the "insufficient evidence" report state — the refusal rendered as a real product frame, presented like an exhibit, unanimated.
Evidence: the refusal state, plus the banned-signals list rendered as a plain, flat enumeration — no icons, no cards, no grid. A list, like a standard.
What moves: nothing. This is the page's designed dead-stop, and it must be absolute — no fade-ups, no staggered reveals. The scene is simply there when the visitor arrives.
What never moves: all of it. Stillness as integrity, per the motion law: refusals hold still.
Aha interaction: the meta-realization, not a mechanic: "they built a section to advertise what they won't do." If any interaction exists, it is a single quiet expand on the banned list for the thorough reader.
Scroll reveal vs. immediate: everything immediate. Withholding within a scene about transparency would be self-defeating.
Whitespace: vast and symmetrical. The frame should feel like a courtroom, not a brochure.
Pacing: the full stop. The page's silence between movements.
Transition: out of maximum stillness into the page's most dramatic scene — the contrast is the setup. A slow scroll-linked dissolve, the field warming by a degree, into Scene 6.

Scene 6 — Inside the Interview
First noticed: a question — set enormous, alone: the generic "before" question every engineering leader has asked a hundred times. Recognition is instant and slightly uncomfortable.
Focal point: the transformation. On scroll, the generic question recedes — smaller, dimmer, pushed up-frame — and the "after" question takes the stage: larger, closer, its factual payload (the service, the date, the latency figure) rendered in mono with the evidence accent. The specificity should feel almost intrusive — how does it know that? — and then the answer is obvious: the record.
Emotional job: desire. The page's emotional peak. Every scene before this earned trust; this one makes the visitor want— specifically, to be the interviewer who gets to ask that second question.
Register: dramatic — the one scene allowed theater, because the theater is made entirely of substance.
Typography: the page's boldest typographic moment. Before-question in muted sans; after-question mixing sans frame with mono payload — the two-voice system delivering its punchline. No imagery competes; type is the cinematography.
Product: subtle and secondary: a small, quiet indication that the after-question originates from the report's suggested-focus area — a thin thread from question back to a report fragment. The product is the source, not the star.
Evidence: the mono payload inside the question is the evidence, plus the thread to its report origin.
What moves: one scroll-linked transformation — before yielding to after. Slow, weighted, inevitable; the most cinematic single motion on the page. Nothing else.
What never moves: the after-question once landed. It arrives and holds — the frame the visitor should remember a week later.
Aha interaction: the transformation itself; no clicking required. Optionally, the thread to the report fragment is hoverable — receipts, even here.
Scroll reveal vs. immediate: before-question immediate; after-question strictly scroll-earned. The visitor must perform the shift themselves — old world to new world, by their own hand.
Whitespace: generous; a stage, not a document. Density returns to zero so the words carry everything.
Pacing: the crescendo. Slowest scroll-to-payoff ratio on the page — make them travel for it.
Transition: the after-question's thread motif carries downward, and the palette warms perceptibly — the page's single tonal shift — into the candidate scene.

Scene 7 — The Candidate Holds the Keys
First noticed: a change of perspective: for the first time, the interface on screen belongs to the candidate — their consent view, their repo selection, their preview of their own report.
Focal point: the candidate-side product frame, presented at human scale — smaller, closer, warmer than the employer views. The visual grammar says: this is someone's own desk.
Emotional job: safety and decency. The founder-visitor's last anxiety (candidates will hate this / legal will block this) dissolves not through argument but through seeing the other side of the table treated well.
Register: warm documentary. The one gently human scene — achieved through scale, tone, and the second accent if it survived Creative Direction, never through stock photography.
Typography: softer hierarchy, smaller scale; mono still marks the verifiable (what's shared, what's withheld, the "code never stored" fact rendered as a plain mono statement — a receipt about ourselves).
Product: the consent flow, real: repo-level selection, the preview-before-share state. Showing granular candidate control is the argument; no diagram of a three-step funnel anywhere.
Evidence: the withheld items visible in the consent view — demonstrating that "no" is a real option — and the candidate's preview of their own report.
What moves: one small, human-scaled motion: a repo toggling from shared to withheld, and the downstream report fragment gracefully acknowledging the change. Consent as a live mechanic, shown once.
What never moves: nothing loops, nothing pulses "friendly." Warmth via stillness and scale, not animation.
Aha interaction: the realization inside the artifact: the candidate can see everything and decline anything — no silent mode exists. If interactive, let the visitor flip the consent toggle themselves.
Scroll reveal vs. immediate: the candidate frame immediate; the toggle moment scroll-earned.
Whitespace: moderate — cozier than the austere scenes, still disciplined.
Pacing: the exhale after the peak. Deliberately gentler and quicker; the argument is complete, this scene reassures rather than persuades.
Transition: the frame quietly empties, warmth persisting, into the close — the shortest transition on the page.

Scene 8 — Settle It on Someone You Know
First noticed: almost nothing — and that's the design. One invitation, one action, on a nearly empty field. The page ends the way it began: still, confident, sparse.
Focal point: the single CTA. Nothing competes with it — no footer noise above it, no secondary grid, no last-minute feature reminders.
Emotional job: agency. The visitor leaves the spectator's seat. The feeling on click should be curiosity with a private edge — "let's see what it says about me."
Register: quiet, final. A closing chord, not a sales push.
Typography: one sans line echoing the hero's register (the loop audibly closing), CTA set in the evidence accent's one permitted non-citation use — the action of generating evidence is the only button worthy of the evidence color.
Product: none visible. The product's absence here is confidence — the next artifact the visitor sees should be their own report.
Evidence: withheld entirely. The scene's proposition is that the visitor will create it.
What moves: nothing on entry. One micro-response on hover/press — hairline precision, no celebration.
What never moves: everything else. The page earns the right to end in complete stillness.
Aha interaction: the click itself — the moment the visitor turns the instrument on someone whose ability they already know. The page's entire architecture funnels to this one act of self-verification.
Scroll reveal vs. immediate: all immediate. No tricks at the door.
Whitespace: maximum of the page. Emptier than the hero.
Pacing: full stop. Silence after the final note.
Transition: none. The next experience is the product.

Director's note for the build team: the page's identity lives in three disciplines — (1) each scene owns exactly one motion event, (2) the evidence accent appears only on the checkable, and (3) two frames are perfectly still (Scenes 5 and 8) on purpose. If any implementation pressure erodes one of these — an added ambient effect, a second accent use, a "livelier" refusals section — the blueprint has been broken, not adapted. Build Scene 4's receipt-pull first; it is the hardest, the most important, and the component every other scene borrows from
